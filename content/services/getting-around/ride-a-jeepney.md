# Ride a jeepney in Manila

The jeepney is still how most of Manila gets to work. This page covers the part of a ride that is written down somewhere official — what it costs, what discount you are owed by law, and how to work out where a jeepney is going before you get on. It does not list routes, and the last section explains why.

## What a ride costs

The LTFRB sets jeepney fares nationally, not the city. The current fares were approved on 17 March 2026 and took effect on 19 March 2026:

| Vehicle             | First 4 kilometers | Each succeeding kilometer |
| ------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Traditional jeepney | ₱14                | ₱2                        |
| Modern jeepney      | ₱17                | See note below            |

Fares are charged from where you board, not from the start of the route, so a short hop is the minimum fare.

## The 20% discount is the law, not a favor

Three separate statutes each grant a **20% discount** on jeepney fares:

- **Students** — Republic Act 11314, the Student Fare Discount Act.
- **Senior citizens** — Republic Act 9994, the Expanded Senior Citizens Act of 2010.
- **Persons with disability** — Republic Act 10754, which amended the Magna Carta for Persons with Disability.

You show the ID for whichever applies: a school ID or enrollment form, an OSCA senior citizen ID, or a PWD ID.

The LTFRB has publicly reminded operators and drivers that these discounts are not optional and that refusing one can cost a franchise. If a driver refuses, the complaint goes to the LTFRB, not to the city.

## Reading a route sign

Jeepney routes are named by the LTFRB in a fixed pattern:

> **[one endpoint] – [other endpoint] via [the roads in between]**

A route whose name contains **Ikot** or **Loop** runs a circuit and returns to where it started rather than running end to end.

The signboard on the windshield is the short version of that name, usually the two endpoints and a landmark or two. If the board says a place you recognise but you are not sure it passes yours, ask the driver or the barker before boarding — that is normal and expected, not an imposition.

## Why this page has no route list

Manila's jeepney routes are being reorganised as this is written, and a route list published today would quietly go wrong.

Under the government's PUV Modernization Program, operators had to consolidate into cooperatives, and route rationalization — the redrawing of which routes exist at all — began after that. As of the end of 2023, 555 jeepney routes in Metro Manila had consolidated and 310 had not. The rationalization stage was still reported as delayed in late 2025.

A wrong hotline number wastes a phone call. A wrong route wastes an hour and a fare, and it lands on whoever could least afford either. So this site does not keep a route table it cannot promise is current.

For an actual door-to-door route today, use a live trip planner — [Sakay.ph](https://sakay.ph/) is built specifically for Philippine public transport, and Google Maps covers Metro Manila transit. Both are updated far more often than a volunteer page could be.
