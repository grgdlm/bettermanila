# Renew your permit and pay local business taxes

Every business in Manila renews its mayor's permit and settles local business tax at the start of each year. This page covers the deadlines, what to bring, and how to pay.

---

## Who must renew

All businesses operating in Manila: sole proprietors, partnerships, corporations, NGOs, cooperatives, online sellers with a local presence, and professionals with a business office. Renewal covers the main office **and each branch** separately.

---

## When to renew

These deadlines come from the **Local Government Code (Republic Act 7160)** and apply nationwide, Manila included.

|                           |                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Annual renewal period** | The first **20 days of January**                                                                             |
| **If paying quarterly**   | First quarter falls due during renewal. The rest are due within the first 20 days of April, July and October |
| **If you are late**       | Surcharge of up to **25%** of the tax due, plus interest of up to **2% per month** on the unpaid amount      |

Prepare your documents in December so you can file in the first week of January. The queues at City Hall get worse every day of the renewal window.

## What to bring

**Business documents**

- Last year's mayor's permit and official receipts
- DTI, SEC or CDA registration
- BIR Certificate of Registration, Form 2303
- Lease contract, tax declaration or certificate of occupancy
- Valid ID of the owner, plus an SPA or board resolution if a representative is filing

**Basis for the tax**

- Sworn declaration of gross sales or receipts for the preceding year, or audited financial statements where applicable
- Inventory list and machinery list for the industries that require them

**Clearances**

- Barangay clearance for the business
- Sanitary permit
- Fire Safety Inspection Certificate from the BFP
- Zoning or occupancy updates if you renovated or moved

Which clearances apply depends on the nature, size and risk category of the business. Bring last year's permits so the checker can see what you needed before.

---

## How the assessment works

1. **Pre-screening.** Submit last year's permit and your IDs.
2. **Application and declaration.** Fill in the renewal form and the sworn declaration of gross sales. Declare any change in business name, nature, capitalization, employee count, floor area, machinery or location.
3. **Routing.** The evaluator sends you to barangay, health, zoning, environmental or fire offices for whatever assessments your business needs.
4. **Assessment.** The city computes local business tax, regulatory fees and other charges. You choose annual or quarterly payment.
5. **Payment.** Settle at the City Treasurer's Office or through Go Manila, and keep the official receipt.
6. **Release.** Claim the permit, stickers or plates and any clearances issued with the pack. Post the permit visibly at your place of business.

---

## How your dues are computed

**Local business tax** is based on your gross sales or receipts from the prior year and your business category, at rates set by Manila's own tax ordinance.

**Regulatory and service fees** cover sanitary, zoning, garbage, signage, occupational and fire clearances.

**Other assessments** may apply to delivery vehicles, storage and stockrooms, machinery and special permits.

## Changes you must report

Report any change of address, trade name, ownership structure, business activity, floor area, machinery or employee count.

If you are closing, whether temporarily or for good, **file a business retirement** so tax stops accruing. You will need your inventory and last day of operation, settlement of any delinquencies, and to surrender old permits and plates. A business that simply stops trading without retiring keeps accumulating assessments.

---

## Practical tips

- Bring paper copies and soft copies of everything.
- If you renovated or bought equipment, bring the permits and invoices. It speeds up zoning and fire review.
- Coordinate with your lessor early for building documents such as occupancy and fire compliance.
- Food and health businesses should book sanitary inspections early and check that staff health cards are current.
- If you choose quarterly payment, diary the April, July and October deadlines. Missing one carries the same surcharge as missing January.
