# See the national museums for free

The country's three flagship museums — the National Museum of Fine Arts, the National Museum of Anthropology, and the National Museum of Natural History — stand side by side at the edge of Rizal Park, and admission to all three is **free**. That is the National Museum of the Philippines' own standing policy: "Open to the general public. Admission is FREE."

![A neoclassical National Museum building in Ermita, a long colonnade running the width of its front.](/images/tourism/national-museum.webp 'The National Museum complex in Ermita, at the northeastern edge of Rizal Park.')

## The three museums

| Museum                             | What is inside                                                            |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| National Museum of Fine Arts       | Philippine painting and sculpture, housed in the old Legislative Building |
| National Museum of Anthropology    | The country's archaeological and cultural heritage collections            |
| National Museum of Natural History | Philippine biodiversity, built around the "Tree of Life" structure        |

All three sit on P. Burgos Drive at Rizal Park, a short walk apart.

---

## Walking in vs. booking

- **Individuals, families and small groups** (under 20 people) simply walk in. **Sundays, Mondays and holidays are reserved exclusively for walk-in visitors** — group tours are not scheduled on those days.
- **Groups of 20 to 150** must book in advance through the museum's PAMANA portal ([reservation.nationalmuseum.gov.ph](https://reservation.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/)), for slots Tuesday to Saturday. The museum enforces a strict **no booking, no admission** rule for groups.
- The booking office is open Monday to Friday, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM: (02) 8298-1100 local 3000, or +63 947 997 3520, or reservation@nationalmuseum.gov.ph.

## Worth knowing before you go

![The National Museum of Fine Arts at golden hour, its columned facade lit warm against the evening sky.](/images/tourism/fine-arts-golden-hour.webp 'The National Museum of Fine Arts, housed in the old Legislative Building.')

- All visitors and bags pass security inspection; baggage counters sit at the entrances.
- Wheelchairs can be borrowed free of charge, subject to availability; some galleries have access limits.
- Each building has a clinic with a nurse on duty, and seating in the lobbies and hallways.
- There is a canteen in the Fine Arts building, and restaurants nearby along Taft Avenue, Kalaw Avenue and Orosa Street.
- Parking is limited and first come, first served; buses may only use the drop-off areas.
