# Spend a day at Rizal Park

Rizal Park — Luneta to most Manileños — is the country's best-known public park: open ground stretching from Taft Avenue to Manila Bay, free to enter, and carrying more national history per hectare than anywhere outside Intramuros next door.

---

## What you are standing on

- **The Rizal Monument** holds the remains of José Rizal, executed on this ground on 30 December 1896. The monument is under permanent honor guard, and the daily flag ceremonies at the Independence Flagpole beside it mark the country's official occasions.
- **Kilometer Zero** — the marker from which road distances in the Philippines are measured — stands on the Roxas Boulevard side of the park.
- The park's open fields, fountains and promenades are free and open to the public; it is one of the few large open spaces left in the densest city in the country.

---

## Around the park

- The **three national museums** — Fine Arts, Anthropology, and Natural History — line the park's northeastern edge, and [admission to all three is free](/services/tourism/see-the-national-museums-for-free).
- **Intramuros** begins directly north across Padre Burgos Avenue — [hours and fees here](/services/tourism/visit-intramuros).
- The bay-side promenade along Roxas Boulevard faces the Manila Bay sunset, the city's oldest free spectacle.

## Getting there

The nearest LRT-1 stations are United Nations and Central Terminal, both a short walk from the park's Taft Avenue side. The park sits between Taft Avenue, Padre Burgos Avenue, and Roxas Boulevard — any Taft-bound jeepney or bus passing "Luneta" stops within reach.
