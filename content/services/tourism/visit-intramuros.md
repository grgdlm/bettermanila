# Visit Intramuros, the Walled City

Intramuros is where Manila began: the walled city the Spanish built after the city's founding in 1571, and the one district where four centuries of that history still stand together. This page carries the entrance fees and opening hours as the Intramuros Administration publishes them, so you can plan the visit before you go.

![The stone gateway of Fort Santiago, its carved relief weathered above the arch, framed by the fort's thick walls.](/images/tourism/fort-santiago.webp 'Fort Santiago guards the mouth of the Pasig at the northern corner of the walled city.')

## Hours and fees, per site

The walled district itself is a public neighborhood — walking in costs nothing, and the walls, plazas and streets are open all day. Tickets apply to the individual sites inside, all run by the Intramuros Administration:

| Site                  | Open                                             | Last entry          | Fee                                                   |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Fort Santiago         | Mon–Fri 8:00 AM–10:00 PM, Sat–Sun from 6:00 AM   | 8:00 PM (8:30 wknd) | ₱75; ₱50 for students, senior citizens and PWDs       |
| Baluarte de San Diego | Daily 8:00 AM–5:00 PM                            | 4:00 PM             | Not posted online — see note below                    |
| Casa Manila Museum    | Tue–Sun 9:00 AM–6:00 PM, closed Monday           | 5:00 PM             | Not posted online — see note below                    |
| Museo de Intramuros   | Tue–Sun 9:00 AM–6:00 PM, closed Monday           | 4:30 PM             | Not posted online — see note below                    |
| Centro de Turismo     | Tue–Sun 9:00 AM–6:00 PM; visitor desk open daily | 5:30 PM             | Visitor center free; paid areas ₱150, ₱120 discounted |

The ₱150 Centro de Turismo ticket (₱120 for students, senior citizens and PWDs) covers its paid areas, including the Centro library and mezzanine galleries.

## The free walking tours

The Intramuros Administration runs **free walking tours** of the district, listed on the events calendar at [intramuros.gov.ph](https://intramuros.gov.ph/) — recent schedules have run them on weekend mornings. If the dates fit your plan, this is the best free orientation to the district you can get.

## The two great churches

- **San Agustin Church** is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the Manila component of the "Baroque Churches of the Philippines" inscription, alongside churches in Santa Maria, Paoay and Miag-ao. It is a working Roman Catholic church run by the Augustinians, not an Intramuros Administration site.
- **Manila Cathedral**, on Plaza de Roma, is the seat of the Archdiocese of Manila.

Both hold regular services; entry arrangements and museum fees are set by the churches themselves.

## Getting there

![A garden inside the Intramuros walls, with clipped lawns and palms below the old stone ramparts.](/images/tourism/intramuros-gardens.webp 'Inside the walls. The district itself is a public neighborhood — walking through the streets, plazas and gardens costs nothing.')

Intramuros sits directly across Padre Burgos Avenue from Manila City Hall. The nearest LRT-1 station is Central Terminal; from there it is a short walk past City Hall into the Victoria Street or General Luna gates. Calesas and pedicabs circulate inside the district — agree on the fare before boarding.
