# Manila by the numbers

How many people live in Manila, how crowded is the city really, and how does it rank against the rest of the country? The essential statistics, each figure taken from the government office that publishes it.

---

## How many Manileños are there?

**1,902,590 people** lived in the City of Manila on 1 July 2024, the reference date of the 2024 Census of Population. That makes Manila the second most populous city in the National Capital Region — Quezon City is first with 3.08 million — and home to about one in every seven of the region's 14,001,751 residents.

| Census year | Population of Manila |
| ----------- | -------------------- |
| 2015        | 1,780,148            |
| 2020        | 1,846,513            |
| 2024        | 1,902,590            |

The city is growing, slowly: 0.72 percent a year on average between 2020 and 2024, by the PSA's measure. That is slower than the National Capital Region as a whole, which grew 0.91 percent a year over the same period.

---

## How crowded is Manila?

Very. The PSA puts the city's land area at 24.98 square kilometers, and at the 2020 census that meant **73,920 people for every square kilometer**, up from 71,263 in 2015. Apply the 2024 count to the same land area and the figure reaches about 76,000. That last number is our arithmetic, not a PSA release: the 2024 count divided by the same 24.98 square kilometers.

---

## 897 barangays in 14 districts

Manila has **897 barangays** — more than half of the 1,715 barangays in the entire National Capital Region, spread across its 16 cities and lone municipality. Manila's are known by number rather than by name: the PSA's geographic masterlist reads Barangay 287, Barangay 288, and so on.

The barangays group into the 14 districts Manileños actually navigate by:

One Manileño in three lives in Tondo. Intramuros, the walled city where Manila began, is now the smallest district by population — Tondo holds eighty-five times as many people. The fourteen district counts sum exactly to the citywide 1,902,590.

For elections the city is drawn differently: **six legislative districts**, each sending one representative to Congress and each electing six of the city council's 36 district councilors. Two ex-officio members complete the 38-seat Sangguniang Panlungsod.

## How Manila ranks

The Department of Trade and Industry scores every Philippine city and municipality each year in its Cities and Municipalities Competitiveness Index. In the 2024 results, among the country's highly urbanized cities, Manila placed:

| Measure                 | Manila's rank |
| ----------------------- | ------------- |
| Overall competitiveness | 2nd           |
| Government efficiency   | 1st           |
| Economic dynamism       | 3rd           |
| Infrastructure          | 4th           |
| Innovation              | 4th           |
| Resiliency              | 6th           |

First in the country for government efficiency is the DTI's assessment, not ours. The PSA's geographic masterlist, for its part, classes Manila as a first income class city.

## What the city earns and spends

In 2025 the city government took in **₱19.3 billion** in operating income and spent ₱14.2 billion running its services, on the Bureau of Local Government Finance's preliminary figures. About seven pesos in every ten of that income the city raised itself — business tax and real property tax are the two big earners — with the rest supplied by the national government, mainly as the National Tax Allotment.

| Where the money comes from (FY 2025)                 | ₱ billions |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Business tax                                         | 6.11       |
| Real property tax (incl. the Special Education Fund) | 5.60       |
| Other local taxes                                    | 0.54       |
| Fees, charges and other local income                 | 1.65       |
| National Tax Allotment                               | 5.35       |
| Other shares from national collections               | 0.08       |
| **Total operating income**                           | **19.33**  |

On the spending side, social services took the biggest share at ₱7.48 billion — health alone was ₱3.54 billion — ahead of general administration at ₱4.69 billion, economic services at ₱1.13 billion, and ₱0.91 billion in interest on debt.

The audited picture for 2024: income of ₱21.84 billion against expenses of ₱16.62 billion, and city assets of ₱87.52 billion. The auditor signed off with a qualified opinion — what that means, and what the audit found, is on [Budget and spending records](/government/transparency-documents/budget-and-spending-records). The audited 2024 and preliminary 2025 figures are kept on different accounting bases, so they are close neighbors, not the same measure.

Running all this took **21,074 people** on the city payroll at the end of 2024: 8,214 permanent employees, 9,599 job-order workers, 2,701 contractuals, 500 co-terminus staff, 40 elected officials and 20 consultants.
