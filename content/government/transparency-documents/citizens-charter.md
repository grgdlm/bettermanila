# The Citizen's Charter and your right to a deadline

A Citizen's Charter is not a mission statement. It is a published commitment to how long each government transaction takes, and it is enforceable.

---

## Where Manila's is published

The City of Manila publishes its Citizen's Charter on its transparency page:

- <https://manila.gov.ph/transparency/>

---

## Why it matters

**Republic Act 11032**, the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act, requires every government office to publish a Citizen's Charter setting out, for each service:

- What the service is, and who may apply
- The documents you must submit
- The fees
- **How long it should take**
- The officer accountable for it

The Anti-Red Tape Authority enforces this. The Bureau of Permits cites RA 11032 on its own page.

---

## How to actually use it

1. Before you go, find the transaction in the Charter and note the **stated processing time** and the **document list**.
2. Bring exactly what the list says. An office cannot invent requirements that are not in the Charter.
3. If you are asked for something not listed, or the processing time passes with no explanation, say so, and refer to the Charter.
4. If that gets you nowhere, escalate. The city's **Bilis Kilos Action Center** is the published route for resident concerns: <https://manila.gov.ph/biliskilos/>

The Charter turns "come back tomorrow" from a fact of life into something you can point at a document about.
