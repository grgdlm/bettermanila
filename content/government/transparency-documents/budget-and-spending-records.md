# Where the city publishes its budget and spending

Manila is legally required to publish what it budgets, what it spends, and what an auditor found. Those records sit in four different places. This page is the map.

---

## Start with the city's own transparency page

The City of Manila maintains a transparency page carrying its Citizen's Charter and procurement documents, and linking out to the national disclosure systems:

- <https://manila.gov.ph/transparency/>

It links to the **Philippine Transparency Seal**, **PhilGEPS**, and the **DILG Full Disclosure Policy**.

---

## The four places to look

| What you want                                               | Where it is published                                                                                  | Why there                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Budget, expenditures, procurement plans, quarterly**      | DILG Full Disclosure Policy Portal, <https://fdpp.dilg.gov.ph/>                                        | LGUs are required to post these under DILG Memorandum Circular 2010-083 |
| **Independent audit of city finances**                      | Commission on Audit, <https://www.coa.gov.ph/reports/annual-audit-reports/aar-local-government-units/> | COA audits every LGU. Reports run from 2014 onward                      |
| **Income, real property assessments, debt, disaster funds** | Bureau of Local Government Finance, <https://blgf.gov.ph/lgu-fiscal-data/>                             | BLGF compiles fiscal data for every LGU                                 |
| **Contracts and awards**                                    | PhilGEPS, <https://open.philgeps.gov.ph/>                                                              | The central government procurement portal                               |

---

## How to read them together

The FDPP shows what the city **says** it budgeted and spent. The COA report shows what an independent auditor **found** when they checked. Read in that order, the pair tells you far more than either alone.

If a figure appears in one and not the other, that gap is the story.

---

## A note on access

Several of these government sites block automated access and only respond to a real browser. If a link fails from a script or a feed reader, open it directly in a browser before concluding it is down.
