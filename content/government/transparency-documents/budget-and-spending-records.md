# Where the city publishes its budget and spending

Manila is legally required to publish what it budgets, what it spends, and what an auditor found. Those records sit in four different places. This page is the map.

---

## Start with the city's own transparency page

The City of Manila maintains a transparency page carrying its Citizen's Charter and procurement documents, and linking out to the national disclosure systems:

- <https://manila.gov.ph/transparency/>

It links to the **Philippine Transparency Seal**, **PhilGEPS**, and the **DILG Full Disclosure Policy**.

---

## The four places to look

| What you want                                               | Where it is published                                                                                  | Why there                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Budget, expenditures, procurement plans, quarterly**      | DILG Full Disclosure Policy Portal, <https://fdpp.dilg.gov.ph/>                                        | LGUs are required to post these under DILG Memorandum Circular 2010-083 |
| **Independent audit of city finances**                      | Commission on Audit, <https://www.coa.gov.ph/reports/annual-audit-reports/aar-local-government-units/> | COA audits every LGU. Reports run from 2014 onward                      |
| **Income, real property assessments, debt, disaster funds** | Bureau of Local Government Finance, <https://blgf.gov.ph/lgu-fiscal-data/>                             | BLGF compiles fiscal data for every LGU                                 |
| **Contracts and awards**                                    | PhilGEPS, <https://open.philgeps.gov.ph/>                                                              | The central government procurement portal                               |

---

## How to read them together

The FDPP shows what the city **says** it budgeted and spent. The COA report shows what an independent auditor **found** when they checked. Read in that order, the pair tells you far more than either alone.

If a figure appears in one and not the other, that gap is the story.

---

## A note on access

Several of these government sites block automated access and only respond to a real browser. If a link fails from a script or a feed reader, open it directly in a browser before concluding it is down.

## National government money spent in Manila

Everything above is the city's own money. National agencies also build in Manila, mostly through the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), and that money sits in the national budget rather than the city's. None of the four places above shows it. The BetterManila budget portal does:

- <https://budget.bettermanila.org/>

It lists the FY 2027 projects it attributes to Manila, both as proposed in the National Expenditure Program and as amended in the House budget bill (House Bill 10858), and links every figure to the page of the budget document it came from. A second view shows the enacted national budgets for 2020 to 2026 for the same area. You can search the list, filter it by program, status and DPWH office, and download it as a spreadsheet.

The figures come from the [BetterGov.ph Budget API](https://budget.bettergov.ph/). The portal is maintained by Mark Lapada ([@aeschyllus](https://github.com/aeschyllus)), and its code is open at <https://github.com/aeschyllus/transparency-portal-manila>.

**What counts as Manila there.** The portal takes everything the budget lists under DPWH's North Manila and South Manila District Engineering Offices, then adds lines from four national programs (DPWH, farm-to-market roads, health facilities and irrigation) whose listing names "Manila". That second step also picks up some Metro Manila-wide programs and facilities in other NCR cities, so the portal's totals may include some projects outside Manila city proper. Check where a project is before quoting it.

These are national appropriations, not city spending, so do not add them to the city's figures below.

## What the auditor found for 2024

The Commission on Audit rendered a **qualified opinion** on the city's 2024 financial statements. That is not a failing grade, and it is not an accusation of theft: it means the auditor could vouch for the statements except for specific accounts whose records could not be verified. For 2024 those were:

- A bank balance of ₱8.07 billion whose reliability could not be established, because the treasurer's records, the accountant's ledgers and the banks' confirmed balances did not reconcile;
- Receivables with ₱276 million recorded without supporting detail, ₱511 million in misclassified tax credits, and ₱771 million left unrecorded;
- An inventory balance of ₱310 million whose accuracy could not be determined;
- A construction-in-progress account of ₱4.09 billion that could not be relied on, including ₱2.10 billion in finished projects still booked as unfinished.

COA also reported ₱42.5 million in audit suspensions, ₱67.2 million in disallowances and ₱33.0 million in charges still unsettled at year-end, and that the city implemented 17 of the 27 audit recommendations from prior years.

The full report is on the COA page linked above: choose **2024 → National Capital Region → Cities**, then "Manila City Annual Audit Report 2024" (the complete report) or "Manila City Executive Summary 2024" (a six-page PDF, the source of every figure in this section).
